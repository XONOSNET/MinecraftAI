Basics or real-world hut trials

Basics keeps your existing 150-stage curriculum. Real-world trials use existing natural terrain in dedicated remote plots, with a shared spawn, independent 7 × 7 houses with 5 × 5 wooden floors and glass boundaries. Prepared-camp resets restore the camp, trees and a one-chunk surrounding ring within a 64-block height band. Natural-resource First Night trials preserve groves around smaller house clearings and include a fully restored one-chunk expedition ring (160 × 160 total). Other untouched-terrain trials restore the full reserved area. The first snapshot takes a few minutes; 256 × 256 takes longer than 128 × 128.

First Night adds 19 survival milestones and four support levels, with automatic collect/train/compare cycles. Bots craft an axe early, scout resources, remember walked routes, forage when hungry, cook meals, make a bed home and practice returning safely. Support decreases after two passing student cycles; ordinary hostile mobs appear only at level 3.

Prepared mode clears a 45 × 45 camp and adds harvestable starter trees; the rest of the natural site remains intact. Uncheck it for untouched terrain (experimental and substantially harder). Local route planning guides navigation. Navigation, gathering and shelter approach select their existing specialist policies in policy mode. Crafting, construction and closing the hut door use a scripted teacher in both modes; construction is not yet a learned policy. Teacher runs collect movement and harvesting examples for the matching specialists. Repeating trials restore terrain after each batch, including timed-out attempts. Automatic world learning performs collection, training and paired evaluation for you. Manual trial loops collect examples only; pause them to train explicitly. Training mixes retained earlier examples with new world demonstrations. Timing is observational in world lessons: slower resource trips continue. Only safety limits apply: 20 minutes for early lessons and 40 minutes for survival sequences. Settlement lessons add real harvestable trees after the hut, then teach chest placement, wooden tools, equipping and supply storage. Crafting and storage are scripted teacher actions in both controller modes. Iron expeditions descend five terraces to real coal and iron ore, then climb back and smelt it. Homesteads add shearing, beds, cooked food and full iron armor. A floating bed icon marks each bot’s registered home and respawn point. Home defense introduces one tagged, weaker husk after equipment and home are ready; other hostile mobs are still removed. These are controlled survival lessons.

    Autonomous curriculum

    Train while you are away

    Collect → train → evaluate → keep or restore → advance.

    150 stages cover twelve skill tracks, including weapon equipment, armor, melee timing, shields and monster combat. After completion, fresh-seed review rounds run automatically, retraining skills that need practice. Sessions renew automatically. Temporary memory or connection problems wait and resume after recovery; critical failures still pause safely. Navigation advances at 90%; broader skills at 80%, each on two complete passing tests (three tasks per bot). A saved model that already passes is confirmed on fresh trials before advancing. Candidates that score below the previous model are restored. Cycles renew every 112 cycles or 6 hours. You can pause at any time; Learning plateaus use an automatic refresher and a reduced training rate; temporary resource problems recover automatically; critical or repeated runtime failures pause safely.

            Loading system information...

            Testing does not update the network. Tests scale with the configured bot count: three tasks per bot. Training uses the examples already collected and leaves the bots waiting when it finishes.

            What to expect

            1. Collect examplesThe teacher demonstrates the current task. Their observations and actions become training examples. The network is not learning yet.
            2. Train the networkThe bots stop moving on purpose. The network studies your examples for 100 training passes, then saves a checkpoint.
            3. Test learned behaviorThe bots move using the trained network, without the teacher. Watch how many goals they reach.
            4. Review and repeatCompare the results. More examples may help, but a perfect score on this simple course calls for a harder task.
