# Natural-world hut trials

Use the dashboard’s “Basics or real-world hut trials” panel. Basics resumes the saved curriculum; a world trial saves and pauses that curriculum. Twelve remote natural sites and two area sizes (128 or 256 blocks square) are available. These are separate natural-world samples, not guarantees about particular biomes. A site without suitable exposed ground or accessible logs reports a failed trial.

The first launch copies the site’s blocks into a reserved template region. Later launches restore that template, including harvested trees and constructed huts. Terrain copying covers Y=-64 through 191, uses 16×16×64 clone segments, checks console acknowledgements and removes temporary force loads. Glass walls enclose the trial. The 256-block version uses separate source and template regions from the 128-block version. Preparation takes time and reports percentage completion.

Prepared camp is enabled by default: it clears a 37×37 shelter teaching camp inside the larger natural region, with eight separated home plots and four harvestable starter trees per bot. The remaining terrain retains its natural form. Both controllers receive the same grove and plot cues. Uncheck Prepared camp to use untouched resource distribution and surveyed natural build sites instead; raw terrain remains an experimental generalization test and is not yet a reliable hut teacher. Bots begin together on surveyed safe ground. They harvest and physically collect 16 logs, craft planks and a crafting table, craft a door, place 54 actual plank blocks and incorporate the crafting table as a wall block for a 5×5 hut, close the entrance and verify an intact enclosure for three seconds. No logs or hut blocks are granted to inventories by server commands; prepared mode places actual harvestable trees in the world. Crafting, the building recipe and door handling are scripted teaching scaffolds. The builder may clear leaves within its own hut footprint or to reach nearby wood; it does not flatten terrain or clear arbitrary structures. Earlier raw-terrain trials exposed navigation, foliage and resource-access failures; raw mode remains experimental. Some raw natural sites cannot yet support every bot. Prepared mode supports up to eight bots.

Teacher runs record action examples for the gathering, expedition and shelter specialists. Policy runs use the existing specialists for harvesting and movement, with a bounded A* planner over loaded blocks supplying safe local route cues. The planner avoids water, lava, unloaded ground, large drops and incomplete footholds. It is software assistance, not learned global planning. Crafting and placement remain scripted in both modes, so a completed hut is not a test of an independently learned construction policy. Choose Expedition navigation, Gathering / harvesting or Shelter approach, then Train selected specialist to train that existing policy from its replay. Replays include basics examples as well as new world demonstrations; shelter examples are added only when that approach is used. The in-game menu also offers gathering and navigation training. World trials currently run one measured batch at a time; reset launches the next restored batch. They do not automatically train all specialists or claim unattended general survival.

The first shelter trials start fed, and local hostile removal protects the reserved area, including when bots are paused. Low food/health, missing resources, unsuitable plots, blocked progress or a twenty-minute deadline stop the relevant trial. Ordinary survival elsewhere is unaffected. World results are separate from basic curriculum promotion gates and are retained in data/world-training.json.

In Minecraft, click “Show in-game menu” on the dashboard. Chat buttons select basics, teacher-assisted world trials, policy-navigation world trials or stop. /trigger lab_world set 0 reopens the menu. Mode-changing requests require an online OP level 4 player. No client mod or new dependency is required. Following cameras continue to use the existing native controls; the in-game HUD shows the world phase and hut block count.

Reserved sources start at X=16384 + site_index×4096 (+2048 for the 256-block variant), Z=16384. Template copies are 1024 blocks east. These regions belong to the simulations: terrain and builds inside them are deliberately replaced on reset. Do not use the template plots for player builds. The normal spawn and previous training lanes are outside these regions.

## Validation

The final prepared-camp teacher trial completed seven of eight huts, each with all 55 enclosure blocks, a closed door, three seconds sheltered and full health. The eighth bot reached the movement timeout and was returned to the protected lab. This is a pilot result, not a claim of reliable untouched-world survival or learned construction. See world-hut-native.json; earlier raw-terrain failures are retained separately in world-raw-terrain-native.json.

The native protocol tests also confirmed wood-to-planks crafting, workbench crafting, door crafting, real server-confirmed harvesting and reopening the vanilla chat menu. Automated verification: 83 Python checks and 23 JavaScript checks passed. No production trial grants inventory wood or building blocks.

## Repeating trials

Repeat trials is checked by default. Completed batches save their results, wait ten seconds, then restore the same site with the same size, controller and camp settings. A twenty-minute timeout records unfinished bots as stopped and continues the loop. All assigned bots must reconnect before another batch begins. Stop world trial or Resume basics cancels repetition; terrain-copy failures stop safely instead of retrying indefinitely. Trial numbers continue increasing beyond the retained thirty-result history. Repetition collects teacher examples; model fitting remains an explicit specialist-training action after stopping the loop.

## Bot lighting

All lab bots carry server-managed level-14 light in the overworld. Invisible marker entities own temporary light blocks at head height; the manager updates positions four times per second and sweeps reloaded chunks every two seconds. Only air is replaced: water, plants, existing lights and structures are preserved. Sources can therefore be unavailable when the bot is submerged or its head space is occupied. Old sources are removed after movement/disconnection and during orderly shutdown; unloaded sources are cleaned when their chunks reload. No client mod is needed. A native Mineflayer observer confirmed minecraft:light with level=14 at the bot source position. Three focused automated tests cover ownership, movement, stale/disconnected bots, invalid positions and cleanup.

## Faster prepared-camp resets

After a camp location is known, prepared trials restore all camp chunks plus a full one-chunk ring around them, clipped to the reserved boundary. The copied vertical band starts eight blocks below the camp (clamped to the template range) and spans 64 blocks. Depending on camp alignment, a local reset covers 80–96 blocks along each horizontal axis. A 96 × 96 × 64 region copies 589,824 blocks in eighteen acknowledged batches, versus 4,194,304 blocks in sixty-four batches before. Prepared camps retain the same anchor between resets. Existing history supplies the anchor when upgrading older templates. First snapshots, unknown camp locations and raw-terrain trials still use a full-area copy. Terrain outside the local reset volume retains changes.

Live validation: world batch 78 restored the anchored camp plus surrounding chunks at X=16400..16479, Z=16400..16479, Y=62..125 (409,600 blocks) in 18.6 seconds. All eight bots then entered gathering, with the repeating loop enabled.

## Timing and efficiency selection

World reports now retain phase times, controller activity times, time to first collected log, rapid left/right decision reversals, tree-target changes and route replans. These are wall-clock measurements, including real harvesting/crafting waits; alignment may overlap movement. The dashboard displays live timing and successful finish ranks. Teacher navigation keeps the selected tree column and approach until depleted, obstructed or temporarily excluded, and holds a pickup target while it remains reachable. This addresses repeated nearest-target changes, not a claimed improvement to learned visual recognition.

World timings are observational: historical completion speeds do not shorten resource trips or remove slower bots. Only the existing twenty-minute batch deadline, health/food checks and boundary protections stop gathering attempts; the sixty-second no-progress check is disabled during gathering. The check remains on movement/construction phases. Bot names and policies are never deleted. Terrain restoration time is excluded.

Automated basics candidate comparisons retain success rate as the primary criterion. When success rates tie, at least three fully timed successes on the same successful route identities allow rejecting a candidate whose median successful completion is over 15% slower. Different successful subsets or missing timings skip speed rejection. Better success rates outrank speed. Selection reasons and medians are stored in model evolution. Timings do not modify imitation-loss weights or automatically fit models inside the world loop: that loop still collects teacher examples until explicit specialist training. Shared specialist policies are compared, not an independent neural model per bot.

## Settlement lesson sequence

Settlement is now the default mission; Hut practice only remains selectable. Each bot verifies an enclosed hut, exits through its real door, harvests more wood, returns home, crafts and places a real chest, makes a wooden pickaxe, axe and shovel, deposits eight planks in the chest, equips its axe and closes the hut door. Six milestones are reported to the dashboard and vanilla bossbars. Completion requires actual inventory tools and server-confirmed chest contents; hut completion alone does not finish the sequence or remove the bot from camera cycling.

Prepared camps begin with six four-log trees per bot (extra headroom for missing or shared drops), then replenish two four-log trees per bot in an outer resource row after its hut is verified. Repeated requests do not duplicate resources. Trees remain within the camp and reset ring and do not occupy hut doors or paths. Raw-terrain missions must find their additional wood naturally. Crafting, chest storage and equipment actions are scripted teacher mechanics, not newly trained neural specialists. The existing movement and harvesting policies still collect demonstrations or control those actions in student mode. More basic drills are useful only when student evaluation reveals a specific prerequisite weakness; running basics longer does not itself teach the new recipes.

Settlement validation: the native protocol fixture crafted all three wooden tools, stored eight planks in a real chest and equipped the axe (docs/settlement-native.json). The first full group pilot completed all six lessons for six of eight bots (docs/settlement-world-native.json). Two attempts stopped during gathering under the old inactivity check; the final update removes that gathering cutoff and adds spare starter trees. Those changes do not constitute a verified eight-of-eight result. Automated checks: 93 Python and 28 JavaScript tests passed.

## Quick acquisition and route locking

World gathering now uses an auxiliary 72-ray peripheral scan (24 headings at three elevations, 32-block range) when acquiring a different tree. It only records actual unobstructed log hits. Known camp/resource locations remain teacher planning cues; this is shared controller assistance, not a newly trained vision network or a change to neural input dimensions. The acquired tree and approach remain stable until depleted or excluded as blocked.

During teacher world navigation, the motor tracks the absolute route waypoint every physics tick, pauses forward movement while sharply misaligned, and brakes before passing the waypoint. World aiming permits up to 540 degrees/second versus the normal 180; policy mode receives faster turns but retains its own chosen turn direction. Actual obstacle recovery, clearance checks, mining look locks and stale-command timeouts remain active. Acquisition scans and tree-lock state appear alongside timing metrics. The simulated locked 180-degree turn completed in approximately 0.35 seconds; this is a controller test, not a measured end-to-end speed improvement.

## Native camera keys

While following, Sprint (normally Ctrl) cycles the seven camera views on the currently watched bot. Forward/backward (normally W/S) increase/decrease distance; left/right (normally A/D) decrease/increase height. Each press gives one step; release before the next step. Space still selects the next bot when cycling is enabled. Distance is clamped to 1–16 blocks; height to 2–12. First-person and stationary views keep their native framing, so distance/height adjustments apply to moving external views. View changes preserve the current bot and cycling deadline; distance/height changes do not detach the camera.

A bundled server data pack detects native input edges at 20 Hz; only active observers receive the control tag. The controller polls pending events without logging every input predicate. The in-game menu also supplies five clickable camera buttons, authorized only for the player’s own active session. Vanilla does not transmit raw Enter/arrow keys; players may rebind Sprint to Enter and movement keys to the arrows in Minecraft Controls to get those exact physical bindings. No client mod is installed. Pack format 61 matches the [official Java 1.21.4 technical changes](https://feedback.minecraft.net/hc/en-us/articles/32385811139085-Minecraft-Java-Edition-1-21-4-The-Garden-Awakens).

Native validation: an unprivileged spectator sent real 1.21.4 input flags. Sprint switched orbit to action, forward/backward stepped distance 2→3→2→1 and held at the one-block minimum, left/right stepped height 4→3→4, and a chat trigger switched to first person while retaining LabAI0. Long-held inputs produced one adjustment each. See docs/camera-keys-native.json. Console input parsing normalizes ANSI colors and carriage returns. Automated validation: 73 Python checks and 14 dashboard checks passed.

## Committed world doorway crossing

World hut navigation now supplies real door geometry at the hut's actual height to the same committed doorway controller used in basic trials. It targets the free portion beside the open panel for either hinge, aligns on one approach side, and keeps that crossing direction after passing the plane. Generic route-heading locks and obstacle reversing yield to the doorway controller. A temporary empty grid route does not clear an in-progress crossing. Ordinary doors remain upright; low-clearance checks still control any necessary crouch.

Native survival-client validation crossed a real door with each hinge in both directions: all four attempts crossed the plane exactly once, in 1.6–2.1 seconds in the final verification, without teleporting through the doorway. See docs/world-door-native.json. The isolated fixture was removed and the test player's temporary operator permission was revoked.
