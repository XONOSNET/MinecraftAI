# Match the starting clock for survival comparisons

Terrain and fauna restoration previously left Minecraft's world clock running
from the preceding attempt. A baseline could start during daylight while its
candidate started at night without equipment or shelter. That environmental
difference can dominate survival scores before the policy reaches later lessons.

First Night attempts now set the clock to tick zero after terrain/fauna
preparation and before final hostile cleanup and player deployment. Teacher and
student attempts use the same starting clock. The ordinary day/night cycle
continues throughout the attempt; hostile spawning, survival requirements and
time budgets are unchanged. This changes the shared world's clock, including
what spectators see. It does not make monster encounters or weather identical.

The session and measured comparison record `start_time_of_day`. Selection
rejects mismatched recorded starting clocks. Older records remain unchanged;
unrecorded historical runs cannot be assumed to have had matching daylight.

This service change loads at a planned lab restart after the current comparison,
alongside the pending waterside landing and spectator-vitals display updates.
Deployment must preserve protected models and demonstrations, resume automatic
learning, and verify server readiness, dashboard availability, eight fresh bot
connections and progressing terrain preparation or a running trial.
