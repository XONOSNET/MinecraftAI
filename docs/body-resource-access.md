# Body clearance and harvested wood pickup

Tree access previously checked the eye-to-log ray, but a clear ray did not imply a walkable feet/head corridor. Gather also deferred item collection while any selected-tree logs remained, discarded drops over 1.7m above/below the bot, and requested pickup goals at the bot's current elevation. This could leave wood across body-level foliage or on an elevated block while the bot continued looking at upper logs.

The shared controller now plans bounded local body access through leaves and safe soil. This uses a detached planning view of loaded terrain; virtual routes are never executed. It clears one reachable, visible obstruction at a time, rechecks the real terrain and uses the ordinary navigation motor for the verified approach or final destination. Both feet and head clearance are considered. Tree stances obscured by soft cover can be selected instead of being rejected immediately. The supporting-ground check also now distinguishes the block beneath the bot from a wall touching its feet: the former stays protected, while the latter can be safely cleared.

Nearby available wood takes priority over remaining upper logs. Pickup goals use the drop elevation and actual standing surfaces. When wood rests on foliage, safe cover beneath it can be removed so the item falls into reach. The bot finishes valid airborne steps rather than cancelling them to replan collection. Unreachable drops receive a cooldown, and pursuits have a deadline that requires real movement or clearing progress.

Local body clearing is limited to eight blocks per task and an eight-metre planning reach. It does not remove supporting ground, hard rock, structures, protected home terrain, blocks beside fluids/gravity hazards, or blocks in another miner's claimed area. Reach, visibility, material and safety are rechecked immediately before digging, so a replacement block placed while the bot aims is preserved. Existing sight-corridor and temporary-column access retain their separate limits.

Validation: all 268 JavaScript tests passed, including feet-level cover below an unobstructed eye ray, full head clearance, safe soil versus hard walls, protected and wet terrain, elevated pickup goals, pickup priority while logs remain, landing continuation, pursuit deadlines, and replacement-block safety. The native fixture used real survival physics, leaf destruction and server-confirmed inventory pickups, without writing training examples or granting items to production bots. Starting pressed against a two-high leaf barrier, it cleared both blocks, climbed toward elevated wood and collected it in 2.920s. It then removed foliage supporting a second drop and collected that wood in 1.621s. See `body-pickup-native.json`.

Deployment restarts only the Node controller and begins a fresh comparison at the existing scenario-2, First Night level-1 site. Minecraft, the dashboard, model checkpoints, training data, identities and unrelated changes remain intact. These are shared controller access skills; the native fixtures do not measure neural-policy accuracy.
