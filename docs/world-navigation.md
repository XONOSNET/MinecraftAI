World navigation keeps a home entrance and a complete three-dimensional route for each bot. The route is a shared geometry aid; the student policy still selects its movement, turns and jumps. These helpers do not add neural parameters or imply that the policy independently learned a world map.

The planner accounts for height, requires the correct destination floor, bounds search to loaded terrain and the trial boundary, and checks that descending steps can be climbed in reverse. A cached block search and priority queue bound CPU work. World controllers execute only complete routes; partial paths remain diagnostic results for other callers. A closed hut is approached through its exterior entrance before planning inside it.

Movement validates the immediate route corridor and ground support at physics tick frequency. A one-block descent requires a matching lower waypoint; ordinary forward movement, harvesting mistakes and local reversing cannot intentionally leave the planned ground level. The teacher recognizes verified ascending steps even if its longer head probe sees the following terrace. The student must still choose the jump action. Shared recovery permits that chosen, checked jump rather than backing away from the step.

Failed route searches retry on the configured interval. Remaining route distance measures progress, so moving back and forth does not continually reset the existing global no-progress deadline. Local recovery resets do not erase that deadline. A genuinely disconnected cliff or unloaded route is reported as a failed search, rather than claimed as navigable. The bot stops that attempt at the deadline; the learning loop can continue. No return-home teleport or terrain modification is used by the navigator.

Dashboard trial telemetry shows the height difference from the home entrance, remaining route distance, or a search-in-progress explanation. Navigation telemetry is cleared while performing other work.

Native verification includes a five-block hill with a far-side staircase and the actual stranded position near the camp. Fixtures use the real world controller and normal survival movement. The camp inspection compares the earlier planner from Git revision `f1145b9` and records the actual return walk; it edits no terrain. The isolated hill fixture removes its test structure afterward. See `homing-native.json` and `hill-return-inspection.json` for measured results.
